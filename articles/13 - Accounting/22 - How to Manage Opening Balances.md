# How to Manage Opening Balances

Opening balances represents the **financial position of your business at the time you start using a new accounting system**. The goal is to ensure your books continue seamlessly from the old system (or manual records) into a new one.

  - **Cash and Bank Accounts**

Create a Manual Journal Entry to record balances in cash and bank accounts.

1.  Go to **Journal Entry / General Ledger**.

2.  Create a **new Journal Entry** with the **effective date** (usually the system start date). Debit the relevant cash accounts, and **credit Opening Balance Equity (temporary account).**

**Example:**

| **Account**             | **Debit** | **Credit** |
| ----------------------- | --------- | ---------- |
| Cash on Hand            | 1000      |            |
| Cash in Bank            | 1000      |            |
| Petty Cash              | 1000      |            |
| Opening Balances Equity |           | 3000       |

  - **Accounts Receivable (A/R)**

For unpaid customer invoices at transition date, enter them individually so they can be tracked and collected properly.

1.  Create **Customer Invoices** (not journal entries).

2.  Enter **customer name**, **invoice number**, **date**, and **outstanding balance**.

3.  Post them to the correct **A/R account**.

\[***NOTE***: This ensures that the collection reports remain accurate.\]

  - **Accounts Payable (A/P)**

For unpaid supplier bills, enter them as open supplier invoices/bills.

1.  Create **Supplier Invoices** (Bills).

2.  Enter **supplier name**, **invoice number**, **date**, and **outstanding balance**.

3.  Post them to correct **A/P account**.

\[***NOTE***: This ensures that the vendor aging reports remain accurate.\]

  - **Inventory**

There are two common methods: **Import products with initial quantities & costs** OR create a **Stock Adjustment**.

1.  For the Stock Adjustment method, go to **Inventory Adjustment / Opening Stock.**

2.  **Add** **each product** with its **quantity** **on hand** and **unit cost** as the start date.

3.  The offset account should be **Opening Balance Equity**.

\[***NOTE***: This sets up both quantities and valuation correctly.\]

  - **Fixed Assets**

Record existing assets with their purchase cost and accumulated depreciation.

1.  **Import** or **enter** each **asset in the Fixed Asset Register** (with **cost**, **date**, **useful life**).

**Journal Entry:**

| **Debit**                                | **Credit**                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Asset Account (e.g., Vehicle, Equipment) | Opening Balances Equity (or accumulated depreciation + net value) |

  - **Loans and Liabilities**

Enter outstanding loans, credit cards, and other payables.

1.  Create a **Journal Entry** for each liability account.

2.  Debit **Opening Balance Equity**, Credit the **Loan/Payable account**.

3.  For loans with accrued interest, **separate principal and interest portions**.

  - **Capital and Owner’s Equity**

After all the above, the **Opening Balance Equity** account should balance. Transfer it into **Retained Earnings / Owner’s Equity / Capital Account**.

\[***NOTE***: This clears the temporary equity account.\]

**Other Crucial Areas**

  - **Prepaid Expenses** (e.g., rent or insurance paid in advance).

  - **Accrued Expenses** (e.g., utilities incurred but not yet billed).

  - **Taxes Payable** (VAT, withholding tax, income tax, obligations outstanding).

  - **Deposits & Advances** (customer deposits).
